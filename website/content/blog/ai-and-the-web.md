# The Web's Sustainability Crisis in the Age of AI

## The Silent Disruption

The web is crumbling.

Right now? Quietly. Soon, violently?

Websites are turning from destinations that people visit to databases for LLMs. Their data is
crawled, repackaged, and served directly to you by your favorite LLM provider: OpenAI, Anthropic,
Google, etc. The truth is, this is a well earned transition for the LLM providers. Search was
overdue for an overhaul and now the genie is out of the bottle: LLMs are here to deliver that
overhaul. Why browse the web when an LLM delivers better answers faster? This is the future, ChatGPT
became the second fastest growing product ever (and horribly named) and users aren't going back.

As a user, this probably all sounds great. However, we can't fully turn the page yet. LLMs created a
silent, looming crack in the web that must be addressed. The core business model of the modern web
is **ads**. A lot of websites are free because people look at ads. You get "free" content, website
gets paid, everybody happy-ish. LLMs disrupt this model though. You don't have to browse anymore
when using LLMs (at their current state). No browsing means no ad impressions. No ads means no
revenue. And no revenue means no incentive for people to publish content on the web. No content
means no web.

But this is still fine right? We have the LLMs now, we do not need to rely on the web anymore.
Here's the catch-22: without the web, AI starves. LLMs might be great now, but over time they need
fresh content to stay relevant, but their very use cannibalizes content creation. At the current
state of afairs, LLM adoption erodes the web. And in turn? The LLMs themselves. Without a solution,
the endgame is lose-lose: a stagnant web and obsolete LLMs.

We need a new, sustainable model for both AI and the web. We're already seeing the early signs of
this transition. A bunch of LLM companies are inking one off, private deals with web publishers to
get access to their data (e.g Google and Reddit). That doesn't scale. We need a model that works for
everyone: users, AI, and the web.

## A Sustainable Solution

AI and LLMs don't have to self-sabotage. They can simultaneously improve their services and bolster
the web. How? By flowing money back to the websites whose content they serve.

Here's how a sustainable system might work:

1. You use your favorite LLM, e.g. Claude or ChatGPT to search for a query.
2. It generates an answer with content from websites.
3. The system calculates the value of each website's contributions.
4. The LLM pays each website for its contributions.

LLMs make money with ads and/or subscriptions (e.g. ChatGPT Plus) and share a fair portion of this
income with the websites that provide them content.

Simple. Transparent. Fair.

## The Economics at Play

Let's walk through a hypothetical example.

1. You ask an LLM: "what's the latest on the LA wildfires?"
2. The LLM searches the web, gathers data from multiple websites, and generates an answer for you.
3. The system analyzes the answer and determines:
   - Which websites contributed data, e.g. cnn.com and nbcnews.com.
   - How much content each website contributed to the answer.
4. Then, like an affiliate network calculates commissions, the system calculates each contribution's
   value. E.g. $0.05 to cnn.com and $0.05 to nbcnews.com.
   - (For context, Google makes ~$0.30 in revenue per search in the US.)

Users get fast, accurate answers. LLMs get content. Websites get paid.

Everyone wins.

## The Current Reality

The web has responded to AI like a host fighting a parasite. 85% of Cloudflare-protected websites
now block AI crawlers.

But blocking crawlers hasn't halted LLM adoption. And it won't.

Lawsuits are piling up:

- The New York Times sues OpenAI and Microsoft.
- The Wall Street Journal and The New York Post sue Perplexity.
- The Canadian Press, Torstar, Globe and Mail, Postmedia, and CBC/Radio-Canada sue OpenAI.

But this is beyond legal compliance and settlements. It's about content. LLMs need it. Mountains of
it.

But content isn't free.

## The Path Forward

We need a durable, reciprocal bridge between AI and the web. A bridge that delivers LLMs topical
content reliably — even content gated behind auth or paywalls today — and delivers websites a new,
sustained income stream: data in the age of AI.

Better LLMs from better content.

The industry needs to move beyond acrimony and toward a sustainable ecosystem where websites are
properly compensated for their content, LLMs have the high-quality data they need, and users get the
best possible answers.

This isn't just about saving the web or improving AI—it's about creating a symbiotic relationship
that benefits everyone in the digital ecosystem.
